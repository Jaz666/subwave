## Changes deployed tonight

- `sleeveNotesResearch` again identifies itself as a music researcher for a radio station. It keeps source-backed facts whose airtime value may depend on a particular album or song, while the check makes the editorial decision.
- `sleeveNotesCheck` asks whether a supported fact could become a natural sounding sentence or two. The library artist, album and track are context for that judgment, never evidence. The originating local track is now stored on new jobs (`sleeve_research_jobs.origin_local_track_id`, schema version 23); older jobs use an associated encountered track when available. Jobs without a matching attachment omit that context.
- A later correction tells the checker whether the article is about an artist or album. Album claims are already scoped to their subject album, so a useful album fact should be `ready` even if its Full wording says “the album.” A fact solely about another release cannot be used as a claim for this album. Artist facts needing a named album or song use matching-only scope. A causal story should not be binned merely because it also contains a date or chart detail.

The controller reported `{"status":"on-air"}` after the first deployment and again after the album-context correction. No web rebuild was needed. ## Play-driven research direction and initial implementation (not deployed)

The owner reframed the budget around the roughly three-minute station track: picking and TTS take at least half of that period, so research should be earned by actual artist encounters. On the first encounter, fetch/cache the whole Wikipedia article, identify and rank possible story leads across it, then check the top one or two only if quiet time remains. Each later encounter may check the next ranked lead, keeping accepted/rejected IDs and the source so work is never repeated. An artist rarely played should receive little LLM work; frequent artists can gradually accumulate up to eight firm claims. A claim enters the usable pool only after exact-evidence validation and the airtime check; rejected leads are not retried simply because the artist plays again.

The initial implementation is now in the source tree. A Wikipedia article is indexed in one `sleeveNotesRank` LLM call, yielding up to 16 ranked, evidence-linked leads; the existing deterministic screen rejects unsupported leads. On the first play, the worker checks one lead if indexing finishes within 35 seconds and the station remains quiet, or two if it finishes within 15 seconds. Otherwise it saves the index and waits for another play. Each later play checks one next ranked lead. A 75-second deadline and a quiet-state monitor abort background model work if the DJ becomes busy; an interrupted encounter is consumed rather than retried repeatedly during the same play. Accepted claims are retained immediately and the cursor is saved atomically. Album Wikipedia and Genius biography checks become eligible only after the corresponding artist's eight broadly usable claims are approved or its ranked leads run out, and only album plays **after** that completion grant album work. The article index is an unmeasured experiment: a 24,000-character article may prove too slow or produce too few good leads on the local 8B model. Watch the first live calls before treating this as a quality improvement.

Database schema version 26 adds durable play consumption and a stable artist completion timestamp. A clean database reset is appropriate for the owner's initial live trial because old in-progress Wikipedia rows hold section-based candidate pools from the former workflow. The assistant has not reset the running station database or run Docker/Compose; root `CLAUDE.md` reserves those commands for the owner. Targeted lint and TypeScript typecheck pass; no automated tests have been run.

## 4 October follow-up — bounded Wikipedia section planning (source edits pending deployment)

The first live attempt for Hole hit the 75-second deadline after a roughly 24,000-character, 161-passage article prompt. The 161 passages were sentence-sized evidence units, not 161 selected claims. The one-call full-article index is therefore unsuitable for the station's model and airtime budget.

The new source edit parses Wikipedia's preserved `== heading ==` structure in ordinary code. It skips list-style appendices, puts early-life/formation and artistry/influence prose before long career history, and splits each retained section to at most 6,000 characters. One play indexes one such bounded part only when its saved candidate pool is exhausted, then checks at most one candidate (two only if indexing was fast). The model still ranks possible sparks *within* that part and makes the separate editorial decision. The article source retention cap rises from 24,000 to 250,000 characters so later sections survive storage, but no model call receives the full source. Up to eight leads are requested per part, and eight approved claims remains the article-level ceiling. Ranked candidate positions stay stable as later plays append sections. Empty sections advance without an unnecessary check call.

The Beatles was used as a read-only structural example: its current plain-text extract is 99,410 characters and the planner yields 28 sections, the longest 5,932 characters. Formation prose comes first, then artistry/influence before the remaining history; catalogue, personnel and references are omitted. This is only a structural check, not an LLM quality or timing measurement. Existing cached 24,000-character source documents remain truncated until refetched or the database is reset. Controller TypeScript and targeted lint passed; no automated tests or Docker/Compose commands were run by the assistant. A production controller rebuild is needed before this source edit is live.
