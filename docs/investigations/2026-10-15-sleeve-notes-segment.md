# Sleeve Notes segment — 15 October 2026

Sleeve Notes now has a code-owned optional speech segment. Configure it in Sleeve Notes → Settings → Sleeve Notes segment. It is off by default, with a 60-minute cooldown, co-hosted discussion enabled and all host DJs eligible. The cooldown accepts 5–1440 minutes. A null personaIds list includes all current/future hosts; an empty list deliberately includes none. When discussion is enabled and the show has guests, the host opens and each guest contributes; otherwise the host speaks alone. The host may reply again, so this can be an exchange rather than one speech per person. No editable SKILL.md or separate user-skill installation is involved.

The existing optional segment tick owns scheduling. Station ID/time-call slots, programme exclusions, listener/budget gates, speech frequency and talk placement remain with the existing scheduler. Sleeve Notes receives one equal place alongside eligible skills; unchosen older skills cannot permanently starve it. Fresh changed-weather priority is preserved. A generation pass backs off before another attempt. No additional cron, research job, external article read or track-selection path was added.

Before generating, code reads the actual on-air marker and the retained local source cache. It requires at least two distinct, enabled, source-backed story Sparks relevant to the track, album or artist, selecting at most four. Bare credits do not satisfy the story minimum. Existing scope, provider, exact-fact/topic/relationship novelty and reservation checks apply. The broad entity cooldown used to space short track links is intentionally waived for this longer discussion, allowing new topics after an earlier short artist Spark. Ordinary link selection and its rotation remain unchanged. The segment has its own durable cooldown in the existing claim-use ledger.

A single structured call receives the selected Full wordings, exact evidence, source URLs, work scope and current roster. It is asked for musical observations and a natural discussion, with no invented factual details, personal memories or temporal playback claims. The schema permits only real speaker IDs, requires the host to open and each participating guest to contribute, and bounds the script. Model silence and output without a detected factual relation stand down. The feature supports at most six participating DJs and twelve contributions per segment. Callbacks are provided only for explicit retained connections whose two tracks have confirmed played encounters during the current station-local day. They cross show/session boundaries and four-hour session rolls, but reset at local midnight. The earlier track must have played before the current track began, with future timestamps excluded. At most two distinct earlier tracks are supplied, with their actual play timestamp and station-local time. Day boundaries account for daylight saving and non-whole-hour timezones. Queued tracks never authorize callbacks.

Schema 52 adds nullable host ID/name snapshots to played encounters. The queue captures the on-air host and event time synchronously before the deferred admission write, so a later show change cannot misattribute the play. Historical plays remain usable with an unknown host; no current roster or archive inference backfills them. The prompt allows a brief natural credit to the earlier host, distinguishes their play from the current host’s own play, and prohibits inventing what the earlier DJ said or thought. Hostless plays use neutral station wording.

After generation, source availability, roster, selected host and enablement are checked again. A concurrently reserved track-link fact prevents publication. Notes reserve existing claim-use rows with consumer sleeveNotesSegment; failed publication releases them. The ordinary queue renders and places solo speech or the complete exchange. Per-speaker ledger IDs survive boundary deferral. Only the queue's actual live-edge callback records normalized TTS text, render and air confirmation. Dropped/reaped/unconfirmed clips release their reservations. The Heard on air shelf retains its factual-signal requirement and labels these entries Sleeve Notes segment. A queued or rendered script alone is never a confirmed airing.

Configuration uses a shared schema mirrored to web, strict patch validation, defaults, partial merge and cold-load restoration. The day-wide callback enhancement requires the additive schema 52 migration described above. The prior manual album-request recovery and retirement of operator-led web research remain in place.

## Ongoing, off-the-cuff conversation direction

The operator clarified that broad musical observations from model knowledge (for example, stylistic blending or a sense of innovation) are acceptable. The prompt now allows general musical commentary and critical interpretation around researched anchors. Specific credits, dates, biography, recording events, awards, quotations and artist intentions still require supplied evidence. Actual-play references continue to require confirmed earlierToday data. No new research or track-selection behaviour was added.

The show and presenters are already established: Sleeve Notes now explicitly prohibits greetings, programme openings, guest introductions, scene-setting announcements, previews and sign-offs. Show Brief is framed as the tone/focus of an ongoing show, not a request to repeat its opening. Colleagues can be addressed naturally without announcing their presence.

Co-hosted delivery aims at one worthwhile musical thread, choosing a strong creative detail rather than automatically the first note. Guests are asked to develop the previous contribution, add a perspective or ask a useful question instead of interviewing the host or repeatedly agreeing. Guidance targets about 80–140 words overall and mostly 8–25-word, one-sentence turns, with uneven lengths and optional short reactions. These are editorial targets rather than new hard validation limits. The prompt discourages padded praise, topic-cue questions, polished mini-speeches, staged disagreement, fake spontaneous stumbles/memories and obligatory summaries.

## Heard on air: complete conversations

Heard on air previously displayed each claim-use row's own matched speech line. That is sufficient for ordinary short links but loses the rest of a Sleeve Notes exchange, especially turns that develop the discussion without introducing a separate claim.

Schema 53 adds an optional segment_id to claim uses and the sleeve_segment_lines table. Each newly generated segment gets a unique ID and every contribution carries its ordered index, total count and immutable speaker ID/name through existing queue metadata. Each use is linked to that segment before publication. The post-air callback stores only its actual normalized spoken line after live-edge confirmation, including lines with no attached claim-use IDs. Transcript writes are idempotent and ordered by contribution index. Null confirmations create no transcript entry. Partial airings remain partial; a generated/rendered manifest is never substituted for speech that was heard. Fact detection and feedback remain attached to their original individual claim-use records.

The shelf attaches the segment's confirmed transcript to the relevant story card and displays every contribution with its speaker name. Incomplete new transcripts show X of Y confirmed contributions. Ordinary DJ links and older entries without recoverable history retain their prior speech excerpt. No change was made to source feedback or moderation behaviour.

Read-only compatibility can expand pre-schema-53 segments from saved broadcast turns in the live session or relevant archives. Recovery requires confirmed air timestamps, explicit claim-use anchors matching the ledger's time/text, a single track and synchronous offer batch, unique claims and bounded conversation size. Ambiguous adjacent exchanges, mismatched anchors and unconfirmed lines are not joined. It never substitutes a model response or writes reconstructed evidence to the ledger. Archive I/O is asynchronous, uses the existing cached summary reader, and loads only relevant sessions. Live read-only inspection recovered the complete seven-turn Beck conversation and a three-turn later exchange, with Dante/Verity attribution; an older Blue Monday excerpt had no match in the current session and remains eligible for archive lookup/fallback.
